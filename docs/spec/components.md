# The components

Records the library's component list, with its status and reasoning.

***Decided*** 2026-11-01 by the owner: build now what a normal website obviously needs, the 20% of components that solve 80% of client cases, and leave the fanciest for later. The list comes from common sense, checked against Blueprint's catalogue and Lumos's component list as inspiration; no tie to Lumos follows (principles.md).

Names use Blueprint's word where one exists (names.md). Each component is CSS on the `kn-` roles, an Astro component for Konzilo sites and plain HTML and CSS in the export (tool.md, point 4).

## In the tool and the presentation

***On the owner's word*, 2026-11-03:** both go through the frame first, in a chapter of its own (Header, Footer, Page), and then through every component above, in the presentation's groups: opening, sections, text, buttons, cards, media, finding and forms. A component has no settings of its own in the design: it takes everything from colour, type, space and shape. So its page in the tool says what it is for and holds no control, and the preview shows the real one at its real widths, on a page of its group's components that the tool's build makes and the presentation leaves out. There it stands with every variant a site chooses per use, side by side and each named (the owner, 2026-11-11, replacing his word of 2026-11-05 that each appears once with its variations chosen beside it; tool.md); the presentation shows them as before. The hero is the exception, set in the design (below).

**How breadcrumbs show** (the owner found it missing, 2026-11-03; the assistant chose the details, ***approved* by the owner on 2026-11-10**): the design file's `breadcrumbs` holds whether a trail shows, the mark between steps (slash, chevron, arrow or dot), the first step (the word Home, the site's name, or a house whose word stays for those who hear the page), whether the trail ends with the current page, and on a phone the whole trail, only the way up one level, or as much as fits from the end (below). It has a page in the frame chapter, since it follows the same rule on every page. A site places the Breadcrumbs component by these settings; the start page has none, since a trail of one step emits nothing. **Its place is not a setting**: it follows the page's H1.

**A third choice on a phone** (***decided* by the owner, 2026-11-11**, who found a whole trail wrapping onto a second line as "Home / Services / Planning" then "/ A clear next step" ugly): "I want a third alternative: you get as much as fits, counted from the right, and when no more fits you get an ellipsis at the left edge, so you see there is more but we cannot see it. Something like: … / Services / Planning." `breadcrumbs.narrow` takes `fit` beside `trail` and `parent`; `trail` stays the default, so no design changes, and the tool names it "As much as fits, from the end". The value is named for what it does, as much as fits, and the assistant chose the rest the same day, to be checked by the owner:

- **What shows:** on a window of 40rem or less the trail keeps to one line and to its end. As many whole steps as fit, counted from the last, stand at the start edge, and an ellipsis there says that earlier steps are hidden before them; a step is never cut, except one longer than the whole line, which is cut at its end with an ellipsis of its own. A trail that fits shows whole and start-aligned, as on a wider screen. Right-to-left mirrors it.
- **How, with no script** (the owner's principle: a script in site output is a failure), alike in Firefox, Safari and Chrome: the list is a reversed, wrapping flex row, one line tall with its overflow hidden, whose steps take reverse `order` so it fills from the page's own step and the steps that do not fit wrap out of sight. The ellipsis is the list's own `::after`, which follows the first step's line and shows only while that step is hidden. Text overflow, the other way the assistant tried, hides whole steps too but leaves its ellipsis beside the shown steps and a gap at the edge, so it was set aside.
- **Order, screen readers and keyboard:** the markup and its order are unchanged, so a screen reader hears every step from Home, and the shown steps stand in that order. The hidden steps stay in the page for assistive technology, deliberately, and the ellipsis is not read. A keyboard focus on any link in the trail shows the whole trail, wrapping as `trail` does, so a focused step is always in view; a pointer's focus does not, so nothing moves under a tap.

**The H1, the trail and the hero's heading** (***decided* by the owner, 2026-11-05**, replacing his word of earlier that day that the trail always stands directly under the hero): "The first H1, wherever it ends up, has the breadcrumb above it, as we have said. You can choose to place the H1 in the Hero, you can choose to place the H1 under the Hero. Then, when you have a Hero section at the top, you can choose to give the heading the same look as an H1 or the look of a Display." Of the eyebrow: it must stay.

- **The rule:** a page's first heading is always its H1, and the trail always stands directly before it. From the top the order is always trail, eyebrow, heading, deck, buttons. Without a hero the first heading is the H1, with the trail above it. The start page has no trail.
- **Two independent settings, all four combinations valid and none disabled:** where the page's H1 is, `inputs.hero.h1`, `hero` by default or `below`, and how the hero's heading looks, `inputs.hero.type`, `heading-1` or `display`, which is only a look. With the H1 under the hero, two headings of one size stand close, so Display often reads better; the tool's tooltip says so and prevents nothing.
- **`hero`:** the hero's heading is `<h1>`, the trail is first in the hero's text block, above its eyebrow, and the next section's heading is `<h2>`.
- **`below`:** the hero's text is a plain paragraph with the same look, not a heading, because a lower heading before the H1 reads as the wrong order to screen readers and search engines. Eyebrow, text, deck and button stay in the hero. The trail stands first in the next section, and that section's heading is the `<h1>`, with the H1 look, following `type.heading-1-role`.
- **A Hero further down a page** is an ordinary section: its heading is `<h2>`, and it may look like H1 or Display too.
- **Per page:** `inputs.hero.h1` is the whole site's default. A page departs from it through the Hero's `level`: 1, `false` for no heading (Heading Group then writes a `<p>` in a `<div>`, since an `hgroup` needs a heading), or 2 further down. A page places the trail by this rule itself: in the Hero's `before` slot, or as the first thing in the next section.

## How a component is built

***Built* 2026-11-01:** Body Text, Heading Group, Button and Link Group, Icon, Card and Card Grid. Button and Link Group are two wrappers within one component family. ***Approved* by the owner the same day in the live gallery**, including the choices below and the unplated card's faint hairline, which uses the ordinary decorative border colour.

**The assistant's technical choices, 2026-11-01:**

- **Files:** `src/components/` has one `.astro` wrapper and one CSS file per component, plus `base.css`, `icons.js` and `index.css`. The wrappers have typed props and render semantic HTML; four client runtimes remain, recorded below: Embed's, consent's, and since 2026-11-11 the header's `header-fit.js` and the footer's `footer-fit.js`, by the owner's choice of a script over duplicated markup. Table of Contents, Visitor Filters, Reading Area, Form, Tabs, Choice and Section run none (the owner's principle, 2026-11-10, principles.md); a sticky header also loads a polyfill where the browser lacks a scroll feature (header.md, How it is built). Heading level is a prop; visual type is independent. The site's entry point loads core CSS and component CSS once. The plain HTML export uses `components-css`, which removes imports by combining the same files. Lucide markup is copied locally with its ISC licence and has no runtime dependency.
- **Layers:** both CSS entry points declare `kn-core, kn-base, kn-components`. Generated roles and Surfaces belong to `kn-core`, the small reset and generic type selectors to `kn-base`, and component rules to `kn-components`. Unlayered site CSS can depart from every library default. Component interaction rules beat the Surface's background declaration regardless of specificity, so the core cannot hide a button's hover state.
- **Classes and choices:** `.kn-<component>` and `.kn-<component>-<part>`, in kebab-case. `data-kn-variant` selects mutually exclusive appearances, just as `data-kn-surface` and `data-kn-width` select their own choices. `data-kn-type` selects a whole visual type recipe. All component CSS reads roles, uses logical properties for RTL, and keeps lengths in rem or em except drawn lines, focus rings, frame and shadows.
- **Plated and unplated:** a box whose colour differs from its ground is a plate. A Card with `data-kn-surface` takes inner space from `--kn-space-plate`, a card radius, frame and box shadow. A Card without it inherits, has no padding, frame or shadow, and takes a block-start hairline and component-gap above its content. An unplated picture replaces that hairline. A plated picture is flush to the edges and clipped to the radius.
- **Frame and shadow:** filled buttons take the owner's frame as an inset ring, in their hover fill when the frame is the quiet edge (2026-11-11, generator.md); outline buttons use at least a hairline ring, with the frame's width when larger; arrow links have no box. **An arrow link's focus ring** (the owner found it touching the word, and then asked whether its place was deliberate; ***decided* by the owner, 2026-11-11**): the link keeps 0.375rem at each end, given back by a negative margin, so the ring keeps off the word and the arrow, also when the arrow steps forward on a press, while the word stays at the edge of its column, in line with the text above. The ring therefore reaches 0.375rem past the column's edge, beside boxed buttons too; the owner kept that over a word set in from the edge. Plated cards take the frame and `--kn-shadow-box`, lifting to `--kn-shadow-box-lifted` on linked hover. These are the only box-shadow targets among the first six. Card hover never changes the background or moves the card. ***Replaced* by the owner on 2026-11-12:** a card that leads somewhere and casts a shadow rises a hair under the pointer, and its arrow no longer moves (The owner's review of a site built with the components, below). Further components settle their attachment when built.
- **Links and layout:** link cards stretch their heading link over the card for one tab stop and put focus on the card. Person contacts stay separate ordinary links. Card Grid caps the column count and steps down with intrinsic minimum sizing rather than breakpoints; four columns fall straight to two and then one, never to three and one, and every column's share is a hair under its fraction, which `1fr` gives back, so no browser's rounding pushes the last card to a row of its own (the assistant's choice, 2026-11-10). ***Superseded* for a card series from the directives (owner, 2026-11-10, via the directive list):** there the number of cards sets the columns, three or four, whichever leaves the last row fuller, and two cards stand in two, in place of a count the caller sets. A window too narrow for four makes the same choice between three and two, so four cards fall to two and two, never to three and one; a window too narrow for three takes two, then one (directives.md, `cards`; built 2026-11-10, For the directives). A Button's label wraps, centred, where it is longer than its line, and a word longer than the line breaks too, so a Button never leaves the window or widens its column; the header keeps its items on one line, as its giving way needs (the assistant's choice, 2026-11-10). A Heading Group standing directly in a Band's content, with something after it, has column-gap after it: a band of a heading and its content is a normal composition, as Hero already spaces its own, so the library owns the space and no site or gallery needs a rule for it. A Band whose `contentClass` lays its content out as a grid or flex with a gap of its own adds the two, and sets the margin to zero there (the assistant's choice, 2026-11-10). Body Text supplies typography without setting a width, independently of the Reading Area.

`gallery/` renders the real components with invented English content and inline SVG pictures. Its controls derive and check a new design before replacing one core style element; its built page embeds every stylesheet and script for offline hosting.

***Built* 2026-11-01:** the Page and Band frame, Reading Area (with Aside Block and Sidenote), Figure, Hero, Text and Image, CTA Band and Key Figures. ***Approved* by the owner the same day in the live gallery.** **The assistant's technical choices, 2026-11-01:**

- **Frame markup:** `.kn-page` renders the header slot, one growing `<main>` for the band sequence, and the footer slot. Its opaque page Surface underlies two optional background layers, `--kn-page-fill-light` and `--kn-page-fill-dark`; numeric mode markers inherit from `data-kn-mode` and select the layer, including images. Neither layer is fixed. The Page's optional `shadow` paints the main content edge over the footer. `.kn-band` is a `<section>` with `.kn-band-content` on the shared content rail. `surface` selects a Surface, and `picture` supplies its background image. Identical adjacent kinds, including no kind, join through `+` and `:has()`; the first has no end padding, unless it is a Hero, and the next supplies band-run space.
- **Reading markup:** `.kn-reading-area` contains `.kn-reading-area-flow` and, when supplied, `.kn-reading-area-aside`. `data-kn-side="end|start|none"` selects placement; `data-kn-stack="before|after"` determines actual slot order. An Aside Block is `<aside class="kn-aside-block">`; a Sidenote is `<aside class="kn-sidenote">` immediately after its anchor element inside the flow. Body Text can wrap that flow. A non-Narrow `data-kn-width` marks a Breakout; width-bearing parts inside a note or another Breakout are not separate rows.
- **Placement, in CSS alone** (***rebuilt* 2026-11-10**, replacing `reading-area.js`, which measured and so moved the page after its first paint): one column until the core's media query in rem, worked out from the design, sets `--kn-reading-columns: 2` (page.md, The Reading Area, point 4). Then an area whose flow holds no Sidenote and no Breakout is a grid of Narrow and the Aside, and a sticky block in it stays at the sticky inset, at most as tall as the window below it, scrolling inside itself; an area with Sidenotes or Breakouts is one block formatting context in which the Aside, written first, floats at the top of its side, each Sidenote floats after its anchor and clears the floats before it, the text keeps Narrow at the reading line, and a Breakout, a formatting context with no width of its own, keeps Narrow beside a float and takes the content area elsewhere; a Figure's picture alone reaches the window's edges, by a length it reads from its own width as a container. The area holds the last note. No sticky block where notes or Breakouts reach into the Aside. `schedule.js` stays for consent alone (2026-11-10). Print keeps one column in document order.
- **Media:** `.kn-figure` is `<figure data-kn-width="…">`, with `.kn-figure-media` around an image or the default media slot and an optional `<figcaption>`. Source images require alternative text. Only the media panel takes a Surface; captions inherit the enclosing context, use small and muted text, and return to the reading line at Narrow's measure. ## Typefaces and labels

*Decided* 2026-11-02, after the owner compared nine label treatments live. The inputs are in tool.md.

- One typeface throughout is the norm. Every other need takes one choice or one line of CSS, and each role follows the one before it unless told otherwise:

  | Need | How | Role |
  |---|---|---|
  | One typeface for everything | `typefaces.main` | `--kn-typeface-main` |
  | Code, or labels in a chosen monospace | `typefaces.monospace` | `--kn-typeface-monospace` |
  | Serif text with sans-serif headings | `typefaces.heading` | `--kn-typeface-heading`, follows main |
  | A display face for large text | `typefaces.display` | `--kn-typeface-display`, follows heading |
  | The first heading in another typeface (rare) | Optional `typefaces.heading-1` input or CSS on `:root` | `--kn-typeface-heading-1`, follows heading |
  | The first heading in another colour (rare) | `type.heading-1-color`: `text` or `accent`, or a Surface-scoped CSS override | `--kn-text-heading-1`, follows `--kn-text` by default |

- The first-heading roles belong to the `heading-1` style. A Hero's heading often has the `display` style, and then the display role decides.
- A first-heading colour set to a role stays checked for contrast. A literal colour is not checked, and on `:root` it would apply on dark Surfaces too.
- A label is the small text around a heading: eyebrows, metadata, category pills and the megamenu's numbers. They share `data-kn-type="label"` and change together. Captions, key-figure texts and form help stay small text, and breadcrumbs follow small text in em, a little under it.
- Labels are in capitals, three steps below body text on the type scale unless changed (five until 2026-11-12, below). No text goes below 12px. The design picks one of these recipes, and `type.roles.label` can set weight, spacing and axes on top (tool.md §2):

  | Labels in | Weight | Letter-spacing |
  |---|---|---|
  | The main family (default) | 600 | 0.06em |
  | The monospace family | 500 | 0.04em |

- All text follows the type scale (owner): every text size reads a type role, or follows its own text in em. A test enforces it; the only other sizes are the icons in the Embed's play button and the header's icon buttons.
- A table head is not a label. It has the typeface of the rest of the table, generally the main family, as written and in semi-bold.
- A typeface can carry tuning of its own, in `src/generator/typefaces.js`. Mona Sans takes `ss05` and `ss07` on text from 20px (owner, 2026-10-30): on where a style is always at least 20px, off where it never is, and otherwise from the window where its `clamp()` crosses 20px. Tuning follows the typeface a style has, so it needs the typeface in the design file; a site that changes a typeface in CSS alone also sets that style's `--kn-type-<style>-features`.
- Rejected: the width axis, and stylistic sets for labels. Mona Sans with a width axis weighs about 150 KB more than without, against 30–39 KB for a whole monospace file, and it exists only in some typefaces. Stylistic sets barely show at label sizes.
- `src/fonts/` holds the publishers' files, recompressed to WOFF2 at most and otherwise unmodified, since several reserve their names and a subset could not keep them. A site copies the typefaces its design uses with `konzilo-design fonts` and loads the `fonts.css` written beside them (konzilo.md), and a browser fetches a file only when the page uses that face. The package a site installs holds no fonts.

### Consent requirements

***Decided* by the owner, 2026-11-02**, from the requirements supplied in the review: GDPR articles 4(11) and 7, ePrivacy as implemented by LEK (2022:482), chapter 9, section 28, and PTS guidance. The site owner carries the legal responsibility. This record and the component are not legal advice; legal sources were not independently checked during the offline implementation.

- Accepting and refusing must be equally easy in the same view, with visually equal buttons; accepting must not be made easier to click.
- Consent is voluntary, specific, informed and active, separately per purpose. No preselected optional choices, cookie wall or consent inferred from continued use; “I understand” alone is insufficient.
- Until the visitor chooses, only necessary cookies/storage may be used. Necessary cookies are exempt; statistics are optional, not necessary.
- Information must clearly cover purposes, withdrawal at any time, the actual cookies/services, third parties and onward sharing, each storage period and the types of data collected. It must be complete and easy to understand.
- Withdrawal must be as easy as consent. A visible control on every page reopens choices without searching or scrolling; a footer link also leads to the cookie page.

***Decided* appearance and behaviour, 2026-11-02:** the non-blocking banner is a floating card at bottom inline end, up to 37rem, full width on phones, with panel shadow, border and `surface="page"` by default. Heading and short purpose/withdrawal text precede the optional `policyHref`/`policyLabel`. Full-width Accept all and Reject optional Buttons are identical and filled; Customise choices is outline. Only narrow windows reserve the measured `--kn-consent-height`.

Settings use a native modal with title/close row, retention and withdrawal explanation, and native expandable category rows on alternate fill with borders/control radii. Real checkbox switches use `.kn-switch` and `role="switch"`; optional purposes start off, necessary is on/disabled with “Always on”. Each row shows a service count, description and a services table: name/cookie, provider, third party, purpose (including onward sharing), storage period and data collected. The footer holds equal filled accept/reject Buttons at start and outline Save choices at end.

After a choice a round fixed Lucide cookie Button remains at bottom inline start. While it shows, the footer's last band leaves room for it under its content, 2.75rem and 1rem above and below it, so it never covers the page's end (the assistant's choice, 2026-11-10). `reopenButton` defaults true, and `reopenLabel` supplies its accessible name. Footer `[data-kn-consent-open]` links still reopen settings. `maxAgeDays` defaults to 182; expired or changed-version choices ask again. The main gallery explicitly defers its banner and disables its reopen Button; the frame lab demonstrates the real banner and invented service records. Consuming sites must keep the reopen control and supply their own accurate service catalogue and cookie policy.

## The owner's review of a site built with the components (2026-11-12)

The owner looked through a whole site designed with the components alone and the directives' HTML, and named what was the design system's to put right. Each point below is his, 2026-11-12, unless it says it is the assistant's choice, which he may veto. The header's points are in header.md, the footer's in footer.md, and those on the directives' HTML in directives.md.

- **A card's padding** (his words, translated: "The gaps are right everywhere, but the padding round the text inside a card is too large"): the plate space, `--kn-space-plate`, is step 4 of the space scale, 25–30px at the default density, where it was step 6 with the column gap, 40–50px. Every plate takes it: a card, a content card, a fact box, an Aside Block, a quotation or a table of contents on a Surface, a choice's panel, a figure's tinted media and an embed's consent panel. The gaps keep their steps. Step 4 is the assistant's choice: it stands between the component gap inside a card and the run of bands, and on a card a quarter of a four-column row wide it leaves the text about four fifths of the card.
- **Eyebrows** (his words: "Eyebrows can be one step larger, and the distance between an eyebrow and the heading after it should be smaller. I think the gap above and below the heading is the same now, and that the gap above must be smaller in general"): labels stand three steps below body text by default, where they stood five; every label grows with them, since labels change together (Typefaces and labels, above). With the default scale that is 14px on a phone and 13px on a wide screen, where five steps gave 12px at both, since no text goes below 12px: four steps would still have given 12px on a wide screen, where he saw them, so the default takes the first step that shows there (the assistant's reading). A design that has saved its own step keeps it. In a heading group and in a card an eyebrow stands a quarter of the component gap from its heading, while the heading stands half the gap from its deck in a group and the whole gap from the card's text (the measure is the assistant's choice).
- **One behaviour for cards under the pointer** (his words: "I like the cards whose heading gets an underline. I only want to add that they also, VERY SUBTLY, rise above the surface a touch, which also changes the shadow they cast"): every card that leads somewhere, a Card whose heading's link is its link, a teaser and a Content Card, answers the pointer and a keyboard focus inside it alike: its heading turns `link` and underlined, its shadow lifts from `box` to `box-lifted`, and it rises 0.125rem over the feedback duration. ***The assistant's choices, which he may veto:*** a card rises only where it casts a shadow, so a card without a Surface, and every card in a design without shadows or with the `box` target off, keeps still and answers by its heading alone, since a rise with no shadow under it reads as a jump rather than as depth; and where the visitor asks for reduced motion it does not move, while its shadow still lifts. A Content Row casts no shadow, and keeps still.
- **The mark that a card leads on** (he was unsure; his reasoning: the mark may be a word such as "Read more", an icon, or the word followed by the icon, the three are one idea and should behave alike, and a word should not move, with or without its icon): he left the choice to best practice, and the assistant chose that the mark stays still in all three forms, which he may veto: the card's own rise is the answer to the pointer. The arrow of a card that leads on no longer steps forward; a Content Card's call to action never did. Outside a card, a Button's arrow variant and the lone action that shows more of a series still nudge their arrow forward under the pointer, as before: they are actions, not a card's mark, and are left for the owner to judge.
