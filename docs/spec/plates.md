# Tokens, roles and plates

Records the token, role and plate model, its decisions and open questions.

**Owner (2026-10-31, night):** the page, the header and the footer are the start of a component framework; cards, forms, buttons and the rest follow. All of them use colours in roles, and spacing, shadows, radii and type likewise. He sees three levels: a framework of **tokens** (colours, radii, spacing, font sizes), a level that maps them to what he calls **roles**, and **components** that use only roles, so that changing the brand colour or the type size carries through to every component and still looks sensible. He recalls Material 3 and his own idea of **plates**: text placed on a colour plate says by role that it is text on that plate, and turns light on a dark plate and dark on a light one by itself. He recalled that the assistant once found Google's solution flawed and had a better one. He asked for research and an idea to present on 2026-11-01; components come only after he has settled the idea.

**Research** found the earlier idea: Blueprint's accepted Surface contract (Q314, 2026-09-14: a Surface supplies a complete set of context roles to its subtree, "no `on-` colour pairs exist anywhere") and ADR 0026 (palettes generated at authoring time in OKLCH against contrast targets). The original criticism of Material itself was not recovered. The research surveys DTCG, Material 3, Radix, Carbon, Primer, Polaris, Atlassian, Spectrum/Leonardo, Open Props, Tailwind v4, Lumos, Utopia and Apple. It compares on-pairs with inherited plate contexts and gives 2026 browser support.

***Decided*** 2026-11-01: the owner accepted this proposal and the six recommendations below. Stance: keep his three levels, split the middle one into context roles that a plate supplies and global roles, generate and check colours when the design is set, not in the browser. This is Blueprint's Surface model carried into the new component model, under his word "plate".

1. **Inputs.** The few choices a site makes: brand colours (seeds), grey temperature, typefaces, base font size and ratio at the narrowest and widest window, density, corner profile, depth profile. Everything else follows from them.
2. **Tokens** (values), computed from the inputs: colour ramps (OKLCH, 100–900, solved for contrast); fluid type steps; fluid space steps; radii; the one layer shadow and a card shadow; durations and easing; the layout values Wide, side margin, the measure (Narrow) and the Aside minimum. Components never read tokens.
3. **Roles.**
   - **Context roles**, supplied by the plate underneath: `--background`, `--text`, `--text-muted`, `--border`, `--border-strong`, `--link`, `--accent`, `--focus`, `--success`, `--warning`, `--error` (Blueprint's ten plus an accent), and a polarity marker, light or dark. **Decided with the components, 2026-11-01:** `--kn-button-background`, `--kn-button-text` and `--kn-button-background-hover` complete the context for a filled button on every Surface, including dark and accent. **Decided with the components, 2026-11-02:** `--kn-background-alt` supplies the alternating table-row fill on every Surface in both modes. Its fixed recipe never goes through foreground solving; the existing text, muted text and links are checked over it at 4.5:1 as `text-alt`, `text-muted-alt` and `link-alt`. Recipes and checked pairs are recorded in generator.md.
   - **Global roles**, the same everywhere: type (body, small, lead, label, the heading sizes apart from heading levels, display, mono), space (gap inside a component, column gap, band space, the smaller gap in a run of same-coloured bands, inner space of a coloured box through `--kn-space-plate`, decided with the components on 2026-11-01), shape (control and card radius), depth (the layer shadow), layout (Wide, side margin, Narrow, Aside minimum).
4. **Plates.** Anything that paints its own opaque background is a plate: the page, a band, the header bar, a footer band, a card, a button, the megamenu, the search box. A plate takes one **plate kind**, which sets its background and the complete set of context roles for everything on it. Text on a plate uses `--text`; it is light on a dark plate and dark on a light one without anyone saying so. Content that paints no background inherits; a plate inside a plate starts a new context (a light card in a dark band).
   - Kinds: `page`, `raised` (a card on the page), `tint` (light brand tint), `brand` (dark brand), `inverse` (dark neutral), `accent` (the brand fill, for buttons and small marks), `picture-light` and `picture-dark` (an image with a tested overlay or text plate).
   - Each kind has values for light and dark mode, both set by the generator (page.md, point 1). Some kinds stay dark in both modes (brand, inverse); others follow the mode (page, raised, tint). A plate may set an explicit local mode, for example a light card.
   - The polarity marker feeds the header (transparent over the first band, it takes the band's polarity, now with no separate declaration), one-colour logos (`currentColor`) and `color-scheme`, so browser controls and scrollbars match the plate.
5. **Why not Material's on-pairs.** Material pairs every fill with an on-colour (primary / on-primary). Every piece of text must then know what it stands on and pick the matching on-role; nesting, images and brand colours of middle lightness break it, and the pairs multiply with states. A plate keeps Material's discipline, each kind being a validated pairing, but delivers it as context, so components do not choose. A filled button is a small plate whose checked button roles come from its enclosing Surface.
6. **Generated and checked, not computed live.** When a brand colour changes, the token tool regenerates the ramps in OKLCH against contrast targets, resolves every plate kind, checks a matrix (each plate against text, muted text, links, needed borders, focus and states) against WCAG 2.2, and refuses a set that fails. The CSS holds literal values. Live derivation in CSS (Lumos's `color-mix`, `contrast-color()`) cannot promise contrast; `contrast-color()`, cross-browser since April 2026, gives only black or white.
7. **CSS mechanics.** A plate kind is a class or attribute that redeclares the context roles, which works in every browser; nothing depends on style queries or `if()`. Each plate also sets `color-scheme`. ***Since* 2026-11-10 (the assistant's choice):** a sticky header that is clear over the page's start takes its first band's roles inside a style query on the page's scroll flag, and shows its logo for its polarity by one (header.md, How it is built); container style queries on custom properties are in Gecko, WebKit and Chromium. The plates' own roles still depend on none, save a part written without the ground it stands on, which finds its ground by one (A plate finds its ground, below, 2026-11-12).
8. **Propagation.** Brand colour → ramps → plate kinds → every component. Base size or ratio → type steps → type roles. Density → space steps → space roles. Corner profile → radii. No component changes.
9. **Component tokens** only where a component has a genuinely independent decision; most have none.
10. **Lumos and Blueprint.** Lumos for Astro uses the same mechanism (theme classes); the mechanism is a plain CSS pattern, and the design system has no ties to Lumos (principles.md, *decided* 2026-11-01; Blueprint's Q367), with generated values in place of Lumos's live derivations. Blueprint calls a plate a Surface (Q314, Q370); reuse that English name, with *platta* in Swedish.

**Presented** with a live demonstration: brand colour, mode, type, density and corners driving the plates, and a contrast table. In it a yellow brand turns the dark brand plate olive in dark mode, the known yellow-to-brown effect; worth raising with the plate kinds.

**Decided with the owner** (2026-11-01, all six as recommended in the presentation):

1. **Plate kinds:** `page`, `raised`, `tint`, `brand`, `inverse`, `accent`, `picture-light`, `picture-dark`. Content without its own background inherits and needs no kind.
2. **Dark mode:** `brand`, `inverse` and `accent` stay dark in both modes; `page`, `raised`, the tints and the status kinds follow the mode; a plate may set an explicit local mode. The colours each takes follow one general rule, *decided* by the owner on 2026-11-05 below, in place of any recipe per kind.
3. **Generated, not live:** colours are generated and checked in the design tool, the CSS holds literal values, and the export stops on a failure. Fluid type and space stay explicit CSS relations.
4. **Context roles:** the eleven in plates.md, point 3 (Roles) plus the polarity. Hover, active and other states get checked values when the components are defined. The three button roles and their normal and hover pairs were decided with the first six components on 2026-11-01; the alternate fill and its three text pairs with Table on 2026-11-02. Brand's alternate fill uses primary 900 because primary 700 misses the link target; the foregrounds and target stay unchanged.
5. **Names:** *platta* and *plattsort* in Swedish, Surface in English. The role names here were working names; the name list in names.md settled them on 2026-11-01, with the prefix `kn-`.
6. **Source:** the token tool sets the inputs and runs the generator; its JSON state is the source and CSS the output. A DTCG export only when something needs it.

***Decided*** 2026-11-01, after comparing both side by side in a live page built on the generator:

7. **A tint per brand colour:** besides `tint` (the primary's), `tint-secondary` and `tint-tertiary`, each generated only when that brand colour exists, following the mode like `tint`, with the same recipe on its own colour's ramp, and checked like every kind. Links keep the primary and focus the secondary on all of them. Without them a site with two brand colours has only one tint, and its second colour's cards and callouts fall back to grey.
8. **Calm tints:** the brand ramps' steps 100 and 200, which carry light tint backgrounds and their borders, and step 900, which carries the dark tint background (*decided* later the same day, after the owner saw a loud blue in dark mode), have a chroma ceiling, so a loud seed (a teal, a yellow, a blue) gives a calm tint in both modes, while a muted seed's tints stay as they are. Step 300, a foreground on dark Surfaces, and step 800, the brand Surface, keep the seed's colour.

**A yellow brand's dark steps** (*decided*, owner, 2026-11-05): they stay olive. Every colour follows one rule, with no exception for one hue: lime and orange darken the same way, so any limit would be arbitrary. If it becomes a real problem, the answer is a general choice for any colour, not an exception.

**One rule from light mode to dark mode** (*decided*, owner, 2026-11-05). The owner asked for a general rule that always works, with no special rules for certain kinds of combination: a coloured band in light mode is a coloured band in dark mode, and it still differs from the background. Every colour lies on a scale of steps, and a given step has the same lightness in every colour, because `TARGETS` fix each step's contrast against white. Since 2026-11-11 (below) each mode reads a scale of ten: light mode 100 to 950, as before, and dark mode 100 to 800 with 850 and 975, its own two darkest steps, where until then it read 900 and 950.

1. **The page** is white in light mode and step 975 in dark mode.
2. **Light bands** (`raised`, `tint`, `tint-*`, `status-*`) mirror and keep their family: step 100 in light is step 850 in dark.
3. **Dark bands** (`brand` on 800, `accent` on 700, `inverse` on 950) keep their family and their step, but in dark mode are never darker than step 800, since 975 and 850 are taken by the page and the light bands. The only colour this changes is `inverse` in dark mode: grey 950 becomes grey 800, so a dark band no longer vanishes into a dark page.
4. **All other roles follow the step the background stands on**, on its mode's scale, not the kind of Surface, and a kind contributes only its family: on step 800 the alternate fill is the next step down, 900 in light mode and 850 in dark, the hover fill the darkest, 950 or 975, muted text 200 and the border 700, whatever the kind. Picture Surfaces stand on no step and stay as they are, on the light scale in both modes. The table is in generator.md.
5. **The seed must not erase a band.** The exact seed takes the nearest step of its ramp only if a band on that step would still differ from the page in both modes. Otherwise the solved step stays, and the seed still gives the ramp its hue and chroma.
6. **A check holds it.** The contrast check has a pair, `background-page`, for every band's background against the page in the same mode, with the minimum `BAND_MINIMUM`, 1.1, just below the lowest legitimate values (about 1.11 in light and 1.15 in dark; since dark mode's own steps of 2026-11-11, below, the lowest in dark mode is about 1.24). A design below it fails `check` like any other contrast failure. Picture Surfaces are exempt, and the page cannot differ from itself.

**A blacker dark page and lighter plates** (*decided*, owner, 2026-11-11). In dark mode cards, the tints and the status kinds all but vanished into the page: step 900 at 16 against the page's 950 at 18.5 left a plate about 1.16 from the page. Shown five alternatives computed with the generator's own solving, the owner chose a darker page with lighter plates, in his words (translated): "If you can make the black a little blacker in dark mode without it becoming too heavy and too dark, that is a good strategy, while at the same time making the plates a little lighter." His comparison changed nothing in light mode, so dark mode has two steps of its own, in every colour and with no exception for a hue: 975 at 19.5 for the page and 850 at 13.5 for the light bands, which the one rule above names. Light mode reads the ten steps it always had and keeps every value, checked against v0.2.0 for the example, the test seeds and all 256 greys at three grey temperatures. For a teal brand and a neutral grey the dark page goes from #131313 to #0d0d0d and a raised plate from #222222 to #2e2e2e. Across the test seeds and grey temperatures:

- a light band stands about 1.44 from the dark page instead of 1.16, and muted text on a plate about 6.8 instead of 8;
- a plate's hover fill, 800, and its border stand about 1.23 from it instead of 1.45, as far as a light plate's do in light mode (1.25). A loud blue or purple tint goes as low as 1.13, since its chroma ceiling lifts it a little above its step, as in light mode (1.10). The hover cannot go lighter, since 700 fails `link-hover`, nor darker, since 975 is the page and the alternate fill;
- table stripes on the dark page and on dark plates, 850 and 975 on each other, go from 1.16 to 1.44; on brand and on `inverse` in dark mode, 850 on 800, from about 1.46 to 1.22, 1.13 at the lowest;
- a grey plate stands about 1.23 from the brand band instead of 1.46; brand's hover, 975 on 800, about 1.79 instead of 1.69, accent's, 850 on 700, about 1.67 instead of 2, and the dark page's, 800 on 975, about 1.79 instead of 1.71;
- `BAND_MINIMUM` stays 1.1, now set by light mode alone, and every design passes `check`.

How the new steps meet the seed: it anchors on the light scale exactly as before, guard included, so light mode keeps its steps; that guard still reads 900 and grey 950, which carried dark mode until now. Dark mode's own steps take the seed too where it lies nearest one of them on the dark scale, a near-black seed on 975 and a deep one on 850, unless the light scale already put it on 800, which both modes read and would then hold it twice. A seed held on 800 that is darker than 800's 11 takes 850 down with it, keeping their spacing of 13.5 to 11, so the tint still stands apart from the brand band and from its hover. For such a seed, a navy such as #0b2f6b or a dark grey such as #333333, the light band stands about 1.24 from the dark page, and a grey plate on the brand band, which is the exact seed in both modes, only about 1.05 from it. 900 keeps its chroma ceiling, since light mode reads it as brand's stripes and accent's hover; 850 has the ceiling the dark tint had on 900.

**A foreground holds on every fill it is checked over** (the assistant's fix, 2026-11-05). A seed anchored at the dark end of a step, such as a loud orange on step 300, could pass on a dark background and still miss 4.5:1 on a lighter hover fill, since solving looked only at the background. Solving now takes the worst of the background and the fills the role is checked on, and only a design that used to fail changes.

**The grey Temperature slider** (*decided*, owner, 2026-11-05) has half its former strength: chroma reaches 0.01 at plus and minus 100, so +100 looks like the former +50 and every position stays a grey that cannot be mistaken for the Tint band.

**Still open:** the remaining components in components.md.

**Decided with the components, 2026-11-02 (owner’s review):** `--kn-background-hover` is the fill under the pointer, beyond the alternate fill where contrast permits. `text-hover` and `link-hover` check the existing foregrounds at 4.5:1; they are matrix pairs, not new text/link roles. `--kn-info`, seed `#1d6fd1`, joins success, warning and error at 4.5:1. Status colours are independent of brand seeds. *(Amended 2026-11-13, to fix a warning that could not be told from an amber brand colour's tint: where a brand tint lies almost on the warning's, the warning turns its hue away from it, and only then; generator.md, The warning apart from the brand's tints.)*

Four always-present, mode-following Surface kinds are **decided**: `status-info`, `status-success`, `status-warning`, `status-error`. Each uses its own ramp’s 100 (light) / 900 (dark) background and 600 / 300 accent, with the tint’s other recipes and all ordinary pairs. The accent belongs to that status hue; links remain primary, focus retains the usual rank policy. Filled Callouts select these contexts; outlined Callouts use the surrounding context’s status role.

**The assistant’s technical choice, 2026-11-02:** keep the 4.5:1 hover targets and approved foregrounds. The requested dark 700 hover fills on raised/tint/status kinds and brand miss link contrast at roughly 4:1 across the seed matrix, so use 800; accent’s 600 similarly becomes 700. Other hover recipes remain as requested: page gray-200/800, raised light gray-200, tints light rank-200, inverse gray-800 and pictures gray-200/800. These are fixed fill recipes, never a second foreground-solving pass. Recipes and export guards are documented in generator.md. Since the one rule of 2026-11-05 above, every one of these fills follows the step its background stands on: `inverse`'s hover is gray-800 in light mode and gray-950 in dark mode.

**A light band's muted text in dark mode** (*decided* by the owner, 2026-11-10): grey 300, mirroring grey 600 in light mode, on raised, the tints and the status kinds. Before, it took the band's own family at 300, which on a tint is the colour of the links and the accent, so a deck read as a link. Links, accent and borders keep the band's family.

**A role that does not show** (*decided* by the owner, 2026-11-10): if a role shows in the design tool it stays, and if it does not, it goes. The accent Surface's `border` and `border-strong` (grey 800 on the accent fill, 1.36:1, and its strong twin) show nowhere: in the tool the accent Surface is only a sample tile with no line, and neither a hero, the footer nor the search box can take it. So DESIGN.md and the presentation left both out, by `UNSHOWN_ROLES` in `src/design-md/surfaces.js`. The same day the directive list made `accent` a Surface a hero and a section may take, so the tool offers it for the hero, where an unfilled button draws `border-strong`, and both roles are back in DESIGN.md and the presentation; `UNSHOWN_ROLES` stays for the rule. The emitted CSS always keeps every role, so that a component on an accent band never inherits the page's border colour.

**A plate finds its ground** (*the assistant's technical choice*, 2026-11-12, for the directive components a Konzilo site takes from this package). The design puts some parts on a plate of their own and gives a part the other plate where the band around it already has that kind, so the plate still stands off its ground: `surfaceOf(part, ground)` gives a link card (a teaser among them), a choice's answer and its panel `raised`, and `page` on `raised`; an action card and a fact box `tint`, and `raised` on `tint` (directives.md, The module). Until now whoever wrote the HTML had to know the ground. A part drawn without it, as the core draws a nested part or a post list's teasers into a section's body with no knowledge of the band around them, takes the Surface `surfaceOf` gives without a ground, and would stand `raised` on `raised`. So the stylesheet finds the ground:

1. **The kind inherits.** Each Surface's block in the generated core declares its kind, `--kn-surface-kind: raised` and so on (`:root` is `page`), and a custom property inherits, so every element knows the kind of the nearest Surface around it.
2. **A style query reads it.** For each kind on which a part takes another plate, the core has a rule inside `@container style(--kn-surface-kind: <kind>)` for exactly the parts `surfaceOf` turns there, as directives.md writes them, with that same kind: `:is(.kn-card:has(.kn-card-link), .kn-choice-answer, .kn-choice-panel)[data-kn-surface="raised"]` and `:is(.kn-card:has(.kn-actions), .kn-fact-box)[data-kn-surface="tint"]`. A style query reads an element's nearest ancestor, since every element is a container for one, so it reads the part's parent, whose kind is the ground's. The rule declares everything the other plate's own block declares, its roles, polarity, `color-scheme`, shadows, frame, background and text, and its kind, so the part is that plate. The pairs come from `surfaceOf`'s own table (`sameGroundPlates` in `src/directives/surfaces.js`), so they cannot part from it.
3. **Nested, it holds at any depth.** The part's own kind is the other plate's, so what stands in it finds that plate as its ground: a card in a choice's panel on a `raised` band stands `raised` on the panel, which stands `page`, and a link card in a fact box on `tint` stands `page` on the fact box, which stands `raised`.

**What it leaves alone.** A Surface written for its ground, as every page until now writes it, is never of its ground's kind, since that is what `surfaceOf` gives, so no rule meets it and it looks as it did. Only the parts `surfaceOf` knows turn: any other Surface on a ground of its own kind, such as a header's panel, a callout or an Aside Block, stays as written. A part written with its ground's own kind on purpose reads as one written without its ground, since the HTML is the same. **Contrast** needs no new check: the part takes the other plate's own roles, which the generator checked. **Browsers:** it needs container style queries on custom properties, which Gecko, WebKit and Chromium have; proved, every pair nested three deep on every Surface in light and dark mode, in Playwright's Chromium, Firefox and WebKit (`test/plate-ground.test.js`) and in a released Firefox 160 and a Chromium browser of version 156 (2026-11-12, as header.md, How it is built, says a released browser is driven). A browser without them draws the part as written. **Why not another way:** the HTML a part is drawn in cannot know its band where the core draws it, a script is against the principles (principles.md), and the core's values cannot reach a component stylesheet without a copy of every role, so these are the one rule set of the generated core that names classes: the parts', as directives.md writes them.
