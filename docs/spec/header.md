# The header

Records the header behaviour, layout and items, with their status and reasoning.

**Owner (2026-10-31)**, his description in short:

- **Attachment.** The header is either fixed at the page's top and scrolls out of the window, or *sticky*, his word: always at the window's top, its look and behaviour depending on where the reader is and which way they scrolled.
- **Page start.** At the top, the page's top edge meets a sticky header's bottom edge, like a non-sticky header, or lies under the header and meets the window's top edge. Then the header may be transparent at the top and turn opaque, by a transition, a few pixels down, and stay so; its content colour may change with it, dark to light or light to dark.
- **Sliding away.** A sticky header may slide up out of the window a little way down, and come back when the reader starts scrolling up.
- **Not wanted:** switching to another header, or shrinking it.
- **Layout.** A start-aligned left column and an end-aligned right column; the items in each are laid out horizontally, with one gap common to the header.
- **Items:** identity, menu, language switcher, search icon, CTA button.
- **Identity:** brand plus an optional slogan; the brand is a logo (an image, preferably SVG) and/or a name. **Decided 2026-11-05, replacing his alignments of 2026-10-31:** a logo is a symbol only, so that there is a reason to turn the name on as well, and what the header shows the footer shows too. Logo and name stand side by side, optically centred on each other, or stacked from one start edge. The slogan stands either to the right of logo and name, optically centred with them, or under the name and aligned with it, and under the logo and aligned with it when the name is off. At least one of logo and name is on.
- **Menu:** a link menu, a horizontal row of links that cannot show submenus, or a menu button, a word and/or an icon. The default button is "Menu" with a down arrow; a hamburger icon must be possible.
- **Giving way.** When the columns cannot keep the gap between them without wrapping, the link menu is replaced entirely by the menu button; if that is not enough, the word before the icon goes.
- **Megamenu.** The menu button opens and closes a responsive megamenu: level 1 on top as a heading, level 2 in columns under each, optional descriptions. Static or dynamic content may sit in a row above or below the menu, for example cards above and a contact link below. These rows go when the screen gets too small.
- **Language switcher:** looks like a link of the link menu. With two languages it shows the other; with more, a submenu of languages. Later the same day he replaced this: always a globe, no language named.
- **Search icon:** a magnifier that opens and closes a search box. The solution he wants: a centred dark box under the header, over a blurred page.
- **CTA button:** a button with a call to action, linking to a popup or a page.
- **Arrangements** (his later word, 2026-10-31, replacing the two "common combinations" of his first description): there are only three, and each may show any one or more of its items:
  - A: left identity; right search, language, link menu, menu button, CTA.
  - B: left identity, link menu; right search, language, menu button, CTA.
  - C: left identity, link menu, search; right language, menu button, CTA.

**Proposed definition** (put to the owner 2026-10-31). The owner settled the open points the same day, marked *decided*: the link menu, the hamburger, T, the globe, the giving-way steps and where moved items go. The rest he has seen but not confirmed point by point.

*Behaviour*

1. **Attachment:** `flow` or `sticky`.
   - `flow`: the header is the page's first part; it leaves the window as the reader scrolls down and returns at the top.
   - `sticky`: the header's top edge is at the window's top edge at every scroll position, except while it has slid away.
2. **Page start** (sticky only): `below` or `under`.
   - `below`: at the top, the page begins at the header's bottom edge, the same picture as `flow`.
   - `under`: the page begins at the window's top edge, behind the header. The first band's content then starts below the header, and the first band declares its polarity, light or dark, which the header's top appearance follows. An opaque header over the page's start would only hide it, so `under` always means transparent at the top.
3. **Two appearances** (`under` only). At the top the header is transparent, with content in the first band's foreground. Past a threshold T it takes its own opaque surface and foreground. Background and content colour change together, in one short transition, and change back above T.
   - Everything follows the content colour: the logo must be a one-colour SVG that follows it, or come in two versions. The CTA keeps a button surface that suits each appearance.
   - Opening a panel does not change the appearance, which follows the scroll position only (*decided* 2026-11-01, replacing "an open panel shows the opaque appearance": the owner found the switch to white disturbing, worst for the globe's small list). Over a dark first band the open megamenu then hangs under a dark bar, as under a brand-coloured header. ***Replaced* for the megamenu by the owner on 2026-11-11:** an open megamenu makes the header opaque, in its own surface and foreground, as past T, and closing it makes the header clear again where the scroll position says so. The globe's list still leaves the appearance as it is, and the search box covers the bar with the rest of the window (point 18).
   - The opaque surface is the header's own setting, the page's colour or the brand colour; with the brand colour over a dark first band, only the background fades in and the content stays light.
   - T is a small fixed distance, reached before any of the first band's content reaches the header: 24px (*decided*). In CSS it is 1.5rem, 24px at the default text size, so it grows with a reader's larger text (the assistant's choice, 2026-11-10, under the rule that every length is in rem).
4. **Sliding away** (`autohide`, sticky, with either page start):
   - Scrolling down past the header's own height slides it up out of the window. The first scroll up of more than a few pixels brings it back; a few pixels of jitter are ignored.
   - At the top it always shows. It stays while a panel is open or the keyboard is in it (`:focus-visible`, so a button clicked with the mouse does not hold it), and focus entering it brings it back.
   - With `under`, it keeps its transparent appearance on the way down until it has slid out of sight, changes while hidden, and comes back opaque; at the top it turns transparent again (*decided* 2026-11-01: the owner found the switch from transparent to opaque just before it slid away flickering and disturbing). While it cannot slide away, it turns opaque at T as usual.
   - ***Built* without script, 2026-11-10 (the assistant's choices):** which way the page was last scrolled is the browser's scroll-state query, so the first scroll up of any size brings the header back, with no jitter allowance, and only a relative scroll counts: the wheel, touch, and the arrow, page and space keys. A jump to a place, such as Home, End, an anchor or a focused element brought into view, leaves the direction as it was. Scrolled up again between T and the header's own height, it is opaque; scrolled down there, it is clear again, on its way out.
   - **Nothing of it shows while away** (a fault of 0.5.0, found on a site whose header slides away and casts a shadow: the header moved up by its own height, and its shadow, which reaches below it, left a grey band a few pixels deep at the window's top while the page scrolled down). ***Put right in 0.6.0, the assistant's choices, which the owner may veto:*** a header that has slid away casts no shadow; its shadow fades over the slide, the movement duration, and comes back at once with the header, as it did. Moving it further by the shadow's reach was rejected, since the reach is the design's and the slide would change with it. `test/frame-css.test.js` holds it in every engine, wide and on a phone.
5. **Not supported:** switching to another header; shrinking.
6. **Reduced motion:** the slide and the switch happen without movement.
7. **Nothing hidden behind it:** while a sticky header shows, the page's scroll padding equals its height, so neither focus nor an anchor target ends up under it (WCAG 2.2, 2.4.11). Since 2026-11-10 the padding stays while the header has slid away, since the root scroller cannot be styled by its own scroll-state; a sticky Aside Block or Section head moves up to the window's top while it is away (the assistant's choice).
8. **Shadow and line:** the upper layer casts the shadow, as for the page and the footer (page.md, point 4; footer.md, Attachment). A sticky header casts its shadow only when page content lies under it. A hairline parts header and page only when nothing else does: same colour as the first band, and no shadow. This is a proposal mirroring the footer. **In dark mode** (*decided* by the owner, 2026-11-11, after the header ran into the dark page in the presentation): a black shadow does not show on a near-black page, so the header bar, its open megamenu and the page's edge over the footer keep their hairline beside the shadow. The same day the assistant fixed a case the rule above already meant: a sticky header at the top casts no shadow yet, so over a first band of its own colour it shows the hairline, also while shadows are on.
9. **Print:** `flow`, without the interactive parts.

*Layout*

10. **Width:** the background runs edge to edge; the content lies in the page's content area (page.md, point 2).
11. **Groups and arrangement:** a start group and an end group, in one of the owner's three arrangements A, B or C (above). The arrangement fixes each item's group and place; the header shows any of them, and those left out leave no gap. Each group holds its items in a row with one gap G common to the header, and the groups keep at least G between them. Everything is centred vertically on the bar's centre line, and the bar's height follows its tallest item plus padding.
    - **Read as two choices:** the link menu's side and the search icon's side, where the search icon sits at the start only with the link menu. Without a link menu A and B coincide; without the search icon too, all three do.
    - **What holds in all three:** the identity comes first and the CTA last; the menu button stands just before the CTA; the language switcher is in the end group. In A the link menu stands beside the menu button, so the button takes over where the links were.
    - **Moved items:** search, language and CTA have the same order in all three, so the megamenu's top always reads search field, languages, CTA.
12. **Giving way.** The content never wraps. When it would, the header takes steps in order until it fits, re-evaluated at every width. A step either drops something that is only looks or moves something with a function into the megamenu, so nothing with a function is lost. A step that frees no width, such as a slogan below a wider brand, is skipped.
    1. (*decided*) the slogan goes, before the link menu, so that navigation stays visible longer. A slogan that stayed because it freed no width goes with the name, so it never outlasts it (the assistant's choice, 2026-11-05, ***approved* by the owner on 2026-11-10**);
    2. (*decided*) the name goes, where there is a logo beside it. Without a logo the name never goes; a logotype that contains the name has no separate name, so the step does not apply;
    3. (owner) the link menu is replaced by the menu button;
    4. (owner) the button's word goes, and the chevron becomes a hamburger (*decided*: a lone chevron does not read as a menu);
    5. (*decided*) the language switcher moves into the megamenu;
    6. (*decided*) the CTA moves into the megamenu. With the name gone, a short CTA often stays even at 375;
    7. (*decided*) the search icon moves into the megamenu, as a search field among the moved items.

    Left are the identity and the menu button. If even they do not fit at 320px, the narrowest width WCAG 1.4.10 requires, the identity scales down until the row fits, the last resort (*decided*). This happens only with a long name and no logo, or a wide logotype. It is not the shrinking he rejected: the size follows the width, never the scroll position. The name never wraps and is never cut.

    ***Built* 2026-11-10, measured by a script since 2026-11-11 (How it is built, below):** a logo is at most 14rem wide, keeping its shape, so a wide logotype leaves room for the menu button even in a 20rem window; the owner asked for a fix that keeps the identity legible there. The last resort is one step, to 0.8 of the identity's size. Where even that is not enough, the identity gives up what it must so that the menu button always shows: a logo shrinks within its box, and ***the name wraps to two lines (the owner's decision, 2026-11-11)***, balanced and centred in the bar, and is never cut. The old script shrank it until it fit, which at 320px went past legibility.

*Items*

13. **Identity:** brand plus optional slogan, the brand being logo and/or name. It is required in every arrangement, with at least a logo or a name (*decided*); every other item is optional.
    - **Decided 2026-11-05, replacing the two nested pairs with their axes and alignments of 2026-10-31; validation refuses the old keys.** The order is fixed: logo before name, brand before slogan.
    - **`identity`:** `logo` (the default, so a new design shows only its symbol), `name` or `both`. Three values of one choice, so a state with neither cannot occur and no control needs refusing or disabling. A design with no logo uploaded shows the name.
    - **`brand-axis`:** `row`, logo and name side by side and optically centred on each other, or `column`, logo over name from one start edge. The tool calls them "Side by side" and "Stacked" (***approved* by the owner on 2026-11-10**).
    - **`slogan-place`:** `beside` (the default), to the end side of the brand after a hairline, the logo, the name and the slogan optically centred on one line; or `below`, under the name from its start edge, and under the logo from its start edge when the name is off. With logo and name side by side the logo stands beside the two lines of name and slogan, centred on them. With the brand stacked, the slogan follows it at the same start edge. All of it is logical, so right-to-left mirrors it.
    - **Optical centring:** the optical middle of a line of words is halfway between its baseline and its cap height, and a logo's is the middle of its box, which is why a symbol should fill its box evenly. A line's box runs exactly from baseline to cap height, with a strut of `1cap` on the baseline and half a line of negative margin on the words, so plain centring does the rest and every height in the bar is true. Measured from the ink in Firefox, Chromium and WebKit, in a sans and a serif, at one, two and four device pixels per CSS pixel, the middles agree within 0.7 of a CSS pixel, and a test holds them to one.
    - The whole identity is one link to the start page of the page's language. Its accessible name is the brand's name: the logo's alt text when no name shows, otherwise empty.
14. **Link menu:** a horizontal list of links with no submenus. The current page is marked (`aria-current`).
    - Its links are level 1 of the main menu (*decided*), so the megamenu always holds them and nothing is lost when it gives way.
    - A header with a link menu and no menu button gets one when the link menu gives way.
15. **Menu button:** a word and/or an icon; the default is "Menu" with a chevron that turns when open, the alternative a hamburger. When the word goes, the icon is always the hamburger (*decided*). It carries `aria-controls` and an accessible name when the word has gone. Since 2026-11-10 it carries `aria-haspopup="dialog"` in place of `aria-expanded`: it opens a modal dialog with no script, whose state the browser keeps, and a written `aria-expanded` would go stale (the assistant's choice).
16. **Megamenu:**
    - **Panel:** under the header, background edge to edge, content in the content area, casting its shadow on the page.
    - **Words:** the header's own words, the menu's and the search box's names and buttons, the languages' and the skip link, are the site's to give in its language, each left out the English one (konzilo.md, The header's own words; since after 0.6.1).
    - **Menu:** level 1 as headings, which link to their hub pages and may be numbered ("01 /"). Level 2 sits as links in a column under each heading, each with an optional description. ***The assistant's choices after 0.6.1, which the owner may veto:*** a level-1 entry with nothing under it is no column with nothing in it: it stands as a link in a row after the columns, in the headings' type, unnumbered, and the numbers count the columns; one with neither address nor entries is left out. A level-1 entry's own description shows under its heading, or under its link in the row, as a level-2 entry's does. A site's menu gives the description through `menuProps` (konzilo.md).
    - **Rows:** optional rows above and below, static or dynamic, which go on narrow screens: at 60rem or narrower, where the columns fall to two, since a row above stacked into full-width cards there pushed the menu itself out of the window (the assistant's choice, 2026-11-10).
    - **Columns:** one per level-1 item with entries under it, up to five; then two; then one.
    - **Behaviour:** it locks the page's scroll and scrolls inside when taller than the window. Esc, the button or a click outside closes it, and focus returns to the button. Only one panel is open at a time, and the header stays while it is open.
    - ***Built* 2026-11-10 (the assistant's choice):** a modal `<dialog>` the button opens by the browser's invoker command, which closes on Esc and on a click outside it, the bar included. While it is open the rest of the page is inert, so Tab stays in the panel, where the script let it reach the bar too; to go from the megamenu to the search box takes two clicks, one to close and one to open.
    - **Moved items:** items moved from the bar show at its top (*decided*), before level 1 and before any row above, in the bar's order, in a row that wraps. There the switcher shows the globe followed by every language inline, so no list opens inside the panel; the search icon becomes a search field; the CTA keeps its button look.
17. **Language switcher:** always a globe icon, with no language named, however many languages there are (*decided*), so that it reads as the switcher in every arrangement, also beside the link menu in A.
    - **The list:** the globe opens a small list of every language, each in its own name (`lang`, `hreflang`), with the current one marked (`aria-current`). Each leads to the same page in that language, or to that language's start page where the page is not translated.
    - **Two languages:** the globe opens the list too, with both (*decided*): a globe names no language, so a click straight to the other one would surprise.
    - **Accessible name:** the control and the current language, e.g. "Language: English" (proposed).
18. **Search icon:** a magnifier that toggles the search panel.
    - **Panel:** a centred box under the header, over a blurred page, with the field, a button and a few starting links.
    - **Responsive** (the owner required it, 2026-10-31): the box is as wide as the content area allows, up to 36rem, and never wider than the window. Field and button share a row, the field taking what the button leaves; where the box is too narrow for both (a window of 360px or less), the button goes under the field at full width. Narrow screens get less padding.
    - **Behaviour:** focus moves into the field. Esc, the close button or the icon closes the panel and returns focus to the icon. A search goes to the site's search page with `q`. Since 2026-11-10 the icon is a button that opens the panel, a modal dialog, with no script, so it is no longer a link to that page (the assistant's choice); a click beside the box closes it too.
    - **Since 2026-11-11** (the owner's decisions, below): the whole window behind the box fades, and is blurred where the design says so, the bar and the footer included; the close button is a choice of the design, and Esc and a click outside the box close it either way; without its heading the box keeps room above the field for the close button; and the empty field shows nothing, the site's word for Search, or a magnifier.
19. **CTA button:** a button-styled link to a page, or one that opens a dialog. It is a complete surface of its own in both appearances.
20. **Skip link:** the header begins with "Skip to content", which shows on focus (proposed).

**Open with the owner:** nothing on the header (2026-10-31). For each question he needs the problem stated and a drawn example.

The sample menu has six top items: Products, Services and Markets also stand in the link menu, while Technology, Knowledge and About stand only in the panel. Technology holds Design, Benefits, Specifications and Patents; About holds About us, Career, History, Board of directors, Management team and Contact us; the first three hold made-up items. - ***Decided* by the owner:** as the very last resort a long name wraps to two lines and is never cut (point 12).
- ***Decided* by the owner, "Vi tar ett JavaScript hellre än att dubblera koden":** he would rather have a script than duplicate markup. The bar is written once, and `header-fit.js` measures giving way, so the header has no hidden copies of its bar and search engines read its links once. **Lost without script, or before it has run:** giving way. The bar shows its narrowest step, the identity and the menu button, with every link, the search field, the languages and the CTA in the megamenu; everything still works, and a long name still wraps rather than being cut.

### A hint of the page (2026-11-12)

***Decided* by the owner, 2026-11-12**, from a site built with the components (his words, translated): "Give the header and the megamenu a hint of transparency, so that one only senses that there is something under them, without it disturbing legibility." What the design offered already: a page start under the header, which makes the bar wholly transparent over the first band at the top (point 2), and the search box's blurred page (point 18). What it lacked was a bar that, where it is opaque, still lets the page under it be sensed. ***The assistant's choices, which he may veto:***

- **A setting of the design,** `header.translucent`, off by default, so no design changes. The tool calls it "Translucent", beside "Slides away".
- **What it covers:** the bar, wherever it is not clear, and the panels that hang from it, the megamenu and the language list. The search box keeps its own fade and blur, and a bar clear over the page's start stays clear, with no blur.
- **How much:** legibility first. Each Surface gets from the generator the least opacity, in hundredths, at which every role a layer draws on its background keeps its contrast over any page, from black to white, as a picture's scrim is found: `text`, `text-muted` and `link` at 4.5:1, `border-strong`, `focus` and `button-background` at 3:1. And it is a hint, never more: at least 85% opaque, `TRANSLUCENT_LEAST`, where the roles would allow more. For the page in light mode grey 600's muted text sets it, 88% for every design; in dark mode 85%; the brand Surface, whose muted text and links stand closer to its fill, about 90 to 96%. The CSS gives each Surface `--kn-background-translucent`, its background at that opacity in each mode (generator.md, Surfaces).
- **The blur:** 1rem, `backdrop-filter: blur(1rem)`, so what lies under the bar shows as soft shapes and colour, sensed rather than read; the search box's page is blurred by 0.75rem, where a reader is meant to see the page still there.

**Sliding away at the window's edge** (the owner's header after his review, 2026-11-12: attached to the window's edge, the page below it, sliding away, translucent). Checked by the assistant the same day in Gecko, WebKit and Chromium at 1280 and 390px, on a real page and in `test/frame-css.test.js`: the header slides away on the way down and comes back on the first scroll up; the megamenu and the search box open under the bar it came back with, a wheel over them moves nothing, and closing them leaves the header where it was; on the way down again it slides away. Nothing needed changing. One difference lies in the engines, not here: closed by Esc, Chromium gives the menu button its focus ring back, `:focus-visible`, so the header stays until the focus leaves it, as point 4 says it should while the keyboard is in it; Firefox gives the button focus without the ring, and WebKit, whose buttons take no focus from a click, gives none, so there it slides away at once.

### How it is built

***Rebuilt* 2026-11-10 without script, by the owner's rule of that day (principles.md, HTML and CSS, not JavaScript):** the header's behaviour is HTML and CSS, and looks and behaves the same in Gecko, WebKit and Chromium. A script stands in only as a polyfill, for a feature one engine lacks and every engine can be expected to have within a few years; it lives in a file of its own, loads only where `CSS.supports` says the feature is missing, sets only what the CSS already reads, and says that it goes once every engine has the feature. Two features qualify: scroll-driven animations, which Firefox lacked, and whose values its style queries miss where it runs them (since 0.6.1, Polyfills, below), and scroll-state queries, which Firefox and WebKit lack. `header.js` keeps only what is decided at build time: the arrangements and the giving-way steps. Since 2026-11-11 one inline script measures giving way, by the owner's choice of a script over duplicated markup (above). The assistant's choices:

- **Panels.** The megamenu and the search box are modal `<dialog>`s their buttons open by `commandfor` and `command="show-modal"`, with `closedby="any"`; the language list is a popover (`popovertarget`). In the top layer each is placed absolutely, by the bar or the globe as its anchor (`anchor()`, `position-area`): Firefox 157 places a fixed one at its anchor's unscrolled place. The search field has `autofocus`, and a transparent close button lies behind the box. The root's `overflow` is hidden while a dialog is open. Since 2026-11-11 the search box's `::backdrop` blurs the whole window, bar included, and a layer fixed to the window inside the dialog fades it in the page's colour at 0.62, since the components read roles and write no colour of their own; clicks pass through it to the backdrop. So the dialog itself only fades in and the box slides, as a dialog that moved would hold its fixed layer within itself. The magnifier in an empty field is an icon beside the field, shown while `:placeholder-shown`, so the field has a placeholder of one space. Headless Firefox and WebKit paint no `backdrop-filter`, so the tests read the blur from the computed style there, and the fade from the screen.
- **Giving way** (2026-11-11, replacing the hidden copies of the bar and their container queries of 2026-11-10). The header writes its bar once, with `data-kn-steps`, the steps it can take in order (`givingWaySteps`). `header-fit.js` tries `none` and then each step in one task, before the browser paints, so nothing flickers: it writes the step as `data-kn-step`, measures the visible groups and the gaps between them, and keeps the first at which they are no wider than the bar's room, compared exactly, with no rounding and no allowance, so the bar never overflows; if none fits it writes `scale`, the last resort. Each step taken sets a flag on the header that the rules read. A ResizeObserver on the room and the groups, and the window's resize event, which a change of text size also fires, run it again, and it watches for a header that comes or changes later; it only re-measures when a size has changed. Compared with the hidden copies it replaced, at every 4px from 320 to 1600px, at 16px and 24px text, in all three engines, for five identities and arrangements (9,630 cases), the steps differ in 15, and in each the script takes a step fewer, at a width where the bar fits to the pixel: a copy measured by a container query can come out a fraction of a pixel wider than the bar itself, most in WebKit, which rounds a container's size to its layout unit. Neither ever let the bar overflow.
- **Before the first paint** (2026-11-11, from the Konzilo core's review; the assistant's choices). Loaded as a module, the script ran after the page was parsed, often after it first painted: measured in all three engines with the script delayed by 300ms, a wide window painted the narrowest step for 12 to 19 frames before the right one, and Firefox and Chromium for one frame with no delay at all. So Header.astro writes the script inline, a classic script without its comments (36 lines, about 2.1 kB), as the header's last element but the polyfill loaders, where it fits its own header while the rest of the page is still unparsed (`document.currentScript`). It is wrapped, so several headers on a page share no global names, and it sets up its observers, the resize listener and, once the page is parsed, its watch for later headers only once, behind `window.knHeaderFit`. Measured again, no engine paints a frame without the step, or with another than the one it settles on, wide or narrow. `components/header-fit.js` stays the source, and a site that does not use Header.astro should write it inline right after each header too, not load it as a file, for the same reason. Since the script lays the header out before the first band has been parsed, a header over the page's start first takes the page's colours and then the band's; its colour transitions therefore apply only once the page has scrolled, so none plays as a page loads, and a hover on its CTA at the very top changes at once. **Not covered:** a logo image without `width` and `height` has no width until it has loaded, so where it loads after the first paint the bar may take a step more then; an inline SVG logo, or one whose dimensions are given, has its width as it is parsed.
- **Height.** The bar's height is worked out in CSS from the identity's settings, not measured: the taller of 4rem and the identity with 0.75rem above and below, and the hairline, where a line of words counts as 0.66 of its size from baseline to cap height. Each header has its own, and the root the page header's, for scroll padding, a page start under it and a Hero's share of the screen. The bar keeps it when the name gives way.
- **Sticky and the two appearances.** `position: sticky`. Three flags on the root, each a discrete scroll-driven animation of a custom property over a range twice as long as where it turns, say whether content lies under the header, whether T is passed and whether the header's own height is. Container style queries read them: under the page's start and short of T, unless its megamenu is open (since 2026-11-11), the Page sets `--kn-header-clear`, and the generated core gives the header the first band's Surface roles and mode while it is set (generator.md); past it, the shadow, or the hairline where the shadow is none. The logo shown follows the header's resolved `--kn-polarity` by a style query.
- **Sliding away.** A scroll-state container query on the root scroller, `scrolled: block-end`, with the third flag, slides the header out unless `:has(:modal, :popover-open, :focus-visible)`.
- **Translucent** (2026-11-12). `data-kn-translucent="true"` on the header gives the bar, outside `@container style(--kn-header-clear: 1)`, and the open megamenu and language list `background: var(--kn-background-translucent)` and `backdrop-filter: blur(1rem)`, which print leaves out. Headless Firefox and WebKit paint no `backdrop-filter`, so the tests read it from the computed style there.
- **Polyfills.** `scroll-timeline-polyfill.js` (44 lines) sets the three flags on the root as the page scrolls, where `CSS.supports('animation-timeline: scroll()')` is false, so in the tests' Firefox, and since 0.6.1 also where the browser's style queries miss what a scroll-driven animation sets, so in Firefox 160 (below); where the browser lacks scroll timelines, it also sets the footer's `--kn-footer-covered` on the Page (since 2026-11-12, footer.md, Attachment). It sets them with priority (`important`), so that a native animation of the same flag, which runs on unseen, gives way to them. `scroll-state-polyfill.js` (26 lines) writes the direction of the last relative scroll on the root as `data-kn-scrolled`, which parallel selectors read, where `CSS.supports('container-type: scroll-state')` is false, so in Firefox and WebKit. A sticky header writes an inline loader for the first, and one that slides away for the second too, each testing the feature and only then importing the file; the first's loader runs the polyfill's own test of style queries, word for word, which a test holds. A footer at the edge of the screen writes the first's loader with `CSS.supports` alone, since its edge reads no flag where the browser has scroll timelines. A Chromium or WebKit page fetches neither; every header carries `header-fit.js` inline (above).
- **Style queries that miss an animation** (a fault of 0.6.0 in a released Firefox, found 2026-11-12 on a site whose header is attached to the window's edge, below the page's start, slides away and is translucent). Firefox 160 runs scroll-driven animations, but a container style query there keeps what it found when its element was last matched, and a flag an animation turns later does not match it again (footer.md, Attachment, has the minimal page). So in Firefox 160 a sticky header's look did not follow the scrolling: brought back to the top by a jump, it kept the shadow it had while scrolled, and it never slid away; on the test pages it cast no shadow once scrolled, and over the page's start it stayed clear past T. Where it did change, another change had happened to match it again, such as the scroll-state polyfill's attribute. The tests' Firefox, 157 as Playwright ships it, has scroll-driven animations turned off, so it took the polyfill and the tests never saw Firefox's own. ***Put right in 0.6.1; the assistant's choices, which the owner may veto:*** the scroll timelines' polyfill also stands in where a short test finds that a style query misses a custom property an animation sets on its container: a `span` of its own with a shadow tree, whose constructed stylesheet a policy on inline styles does not govern, an element there whose CSS animation sets `--f`, and its child, whose `@container style(--f: 1)` sets `--g`; the child's `--g` is read and the span removed at once. It reads 1 in Chromium, WebKit and Safari, and nothing in Firefox, 157 and 160, with scroll timelines on or off. **Not done in CSS alone:** the header over the page's start takes the first band's colours from the generated core by a style query, which no value an animation sets can reach in Firefox, and it slides by a transition, which Firefox starts from no animated value; so the header in Firefox needs the polyfill, as in the tests' Firefox before. `test/frame-css.test.js` runs every test in Firefox with its own scroll-driven animations turned on too (`layout.css.scroll-driven-animations.enabled`), which goes red on this fault, and holds a sticky header's shadow: none at the top, its shadow once scrolled, none again back at the top by a jump and by Home, wide, in every engine. Delete the test once every engine's style queries follow an animation, with the polyfill.
- **Checked in released browsers** (2026-11-12). Playwright's engines are not the browsers people run: its Firefox lacked scroll timelines, and Safari's engine may differ from its WebKit. A released browser can be driven from Node: an installed Firefox by `puppeteer-core` over WebDriver BiDi (`puppeteer.launch({ browser: 'firefox', executablePath, protocol: 'webDriverBiDi', headless: true })`, a profile of its own), a Chromium-based browser by `puppeteer-core` with its `executablePath`, and Safari through `safaridriver` and the W3C WebDriver protocol, once Safari allows remote automation; its driver applies a wheel's delta from a fixed point rather than from where the page is, so Safari is scrolled by its arrow keys, one action each, which count as relative scrolls too, and its screenshots are at the screen's pixel ratio. A page built with the components is served from a local folder, scrolled by the wheel, a little before the end and to the very end, and read by computed style (the flags, the bar's and the page's main's `box-shadow`, the header's position) and by screenshot pixels, such as a column from the page's edge down over the side margin, where a shadow shows as rows darker than the footer's own colour further down. The two-element page above tells in a moment whether a browser's style queries follow an animation. Seen so: Firefox 160, Safari 26.6 and a Chromium browser of version 156 as released, at 2576 by 959, 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, on a long page and a short one.
- **Without script.** The bar shows its narrowest step (the owner, 2026-11-11), and everything else works but what a polyfill adds: in a Firefox without scroll timelines a sticky header is opaque with its shadow from the start and does not slide away, and in WebKit it does not slide away. **Seen, not changed, for the owner (2026-11-12):** Firefox 160 without script runs the animations, but its style queries miss them, so a sticky header keeps the look it had as the page loaded: one below the page's start casts no shadow once scrolled, and one over it stays clear, in the first band's colours, over the content scrolled under it. CSS cannot tell such a browser from one whose queries follow, so only giving every browser without script the opaque header with its shadow from the start would end it, at the cost of the header's states without script in Chromium and WebKit. The panels and the stickiness need no script anywhere.
- **What differs from the script, everywhere alike:** a scroll up of any size brings a hidden header back, and only a relative scroll moves it (point 4); the scroll padding stays while it is away (point 7); Tab stays in an open panel, and from one panel to another takes two clicks (point 16); the bar keeps its height when the name gives way; the last resort is one step, and then a name on two lines (point 12). `scrollBy()` from a page's own script counts as a relative scroll in Chromium and not in the polyfill.
